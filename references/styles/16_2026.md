# 16 · Contemporary flat illustration with grain (Dribbble-style editorial vector, 2020s)

**Status:** Built ★★★
**Layer:** Material (works on any subject)
**Web fit:** High — product UIs, SaaS marketing, onboarding, empty states and spot illustrations; it is the native idiom of current web illustration. Risk: it is so common it can read as generic unless the grain, palette and acting are done with care.

## Recognisable traits

- Pure flat colour blocks with no outlines at all; shapes meet edge to edge.
- Volume only from a block one step darker in the same hue (a diagonal light plane on a sweater, a darker arc on a thigh), never from gradients.
- Stippled grain: tiny square dots whose density is a function of position, thickening toward one side of each shape, like risograph or screen-print texture.
- Big-head, rounded-limb proportions for figures (the source scales heads ×1.2 around the neck).
- Lively secondary motion: lamps swing, plants wobble, little decorative symbols "boil" around the scene.

Relationship: warm, slightly muted mid-tones (coral, amber, pink, lilac, sage), each with one darker partner for its shadow plane. No pure black shapes, no outlines, no gradients inside shapes. Dark reading (suggested): a deep umber or charcoal ground, the same mid-tones a step lighter, grain switched to light dots.

## Typography

A friendly geometric or grotesque sans for headings, suggested **Bricolage Grotesque** 700 or **Geist** 700–800 (`"Bricolage Grotesque", "Geist", system-ui, sans-serif`), tight tracking (−0.01em). Body in the same family at 400. A handwritten script (the source signs the piece in **Caveat** 600) for a single signature or annotation, never paragraphs.

## Surfaces, texture and edges

- **Grain tile (from the app's paper look):** a 90×90 SVG `feTurbulence` (`fractalNoise`, `baseFrequency` 1.4) pushed through a `feColorMatrix` whose alpha row is steep (e.g. `0 0 0 -6 2.6`) so noise thresholds into crisp dots. Make one dark-ink tile and one light tile; inline them as `data:` URLs.
- **Density gradient:** layer the grain under a gradient of the surface colour so the dots show only toward one corner: `background-image: linear-gradient(to bottom right, var(--fill) 45%, transparent 100%), var(--grain);`. The source densities: a pink circle `0.12 + 0.75·clamp((y − cy + 120)/420)` (denser toward the bottom); lilac blocks denser to the bottom-right.
- **Grain only on illustrations:** the source applies a static grain texture with `source-atop`, so grain falls only on figures. In CSS, `mask` the grain layer with the illustration, or put the grain inside the SVG with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` clipped to the shapes.
- **Light cones:** a pre-rendered sprite of dots (26,000 in a 340×420 tile in the source) widening downward and fading, denser at the centre; swing it with its lamp.
- Edges are crisp vector edges. No cut-paper jitter (that is Matisse).

## Shapes and layout

Generous corner radius or soft organic blobs; no strokes. Shadows, if any, are flat offset blobs in a darker tone of the ground, not blurred. Compositions are airy: a big circle or blob behind a figure, a few floating decorative symbols (sparkles, plus signs, dots), lots of ground. For page layout: plain grids with large spot illustrations, blob backgrounds behind section heads.

## Components

- **Buttons:** solid coral or amber fill, dark text, large radius; hover shows a darker same-hue plane sliding in from one corner.
- **Cards:** off-white surface with corner grain; an optional coloured blob peeking from behind.
- **Navigation:** flat pills; the active pill is a mid-tone block with its darker shadow plane.
- **Inputs:** soft-cornered fields, a 2px mid-tone focus ring plus a visible outline.
- **Chat bubbles:** flat rounded blocks in two mid-tones; a "•••" typing bubble that shrinks away when the reply lands.
- **Badges:** small flat blobs; status as tiny spot icons.
- **Loading:** the three dots, or a little character in a typing loop (hands alternating at 22rad/s, 7px lift).
- **Empty / error states:** this is where the style shines: a small scene with one clear action (the cup that falls and breaks, with a stain and shards).
- **Illustrations and mascots:** big head, rounded limbs, simple dot eyes; expressions change in clear steps (closed happy arcs → dot eyes with highlight → wide eyes and open mouth) with an overshoot pop when they switch.

## Motion

The source is mostly character acting; the reusable lessons for UI micro-interactions:

- **Anticipation → action → hold → return:** each tap in the source is 7 frames wind-up, 4 strike, 4 hold, 8 return (at 60fps ≈ 115 / 65 / 65 / 135ms). Use it for "send", "like" and "add" animations.
- **Squash and stretch around the contact point:** crouch `scaleY 1 → 0.86`, `scaleX 1 → 1.07`; take-off stretch `scaleY 1.12`; landing squash `scaleY 0.70`, `scaleX 1.18`, then settle with a small overshoot (0.96 → 1.07 → 1.0). Set `transform-origin` at the bottom so feet don't leave the ground.
- **Snap flip:** when an element must turn around, flip `scaleX` from +1 to −1 on the most squashed frame; cleaner than a 2–3-frame paper turn.
- **Damped secondary motion:** a lamp or icon hit by an event swings `damped(amplitude 0.055rad, period 26 frames, decay 22 frames)`; a plant shakes with a shorter period (9 frames). Trigger these on success or error to make the page react.
- **Ambient:** clouds 15px/s; sun rays rotating 0.35rad/s; decorative symbols boiling at 4fps (±1.5px, ±0.09rad).
- **Nervous lines:** three radiating strokes pop in with overshoot and change colour from pale pink to red as tension rises; good for warnings.
- Easing: `outBack`-style overshoot for pops, damped springs for reactions. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`: no squash, swings or boiling; switch states instantly.

## Techniques

- Pose libraries plus interpolation: store each pose as local keypoints, `lerp` between poses, and apply squash about the feet or the centre depending on the phase. For UI, the same idea is CSS keyframes between a few named states rather than free physics.
- Aim in the transformed space: when an element tilts toward a target, invert the current transform to convert the target into local coordinates; otherwise a 0.38rad lean puts the hand 60px off. On the web: use `getBoundingClientRect` after the transform, or compute targets in the parent's space.
- Signature reveal: write a handwritten word by moving a clip edge across it, with an underline whose stroke tapers 3.4 → 1.4px drawn by the same edge (`stroke-dashoffset` + `clip-path: inset()`).

## Performance and accessibility

- Cheap: SVG illustrations with a cached grain tile; transforms for squash and swing. Expensive: tens of thousands of live dots; pre-render light cones and grain once.
- Contrast: dark text on the surface 15.1:1; dark text on coral 7.0:1 and on amber 8.8:1, so labels on accent buttons pass AA. White on coral or amber fails; keep text dark.
- Grain over text lowers legibility; keep grain off the text area (density gradient pointing away from copy).
- Bouncy acting can distract; limit it to moments users trigger, and honour reduced motion.

## Pitfalls

- Aiming a tilted element at a target with untransformed coordinates → it misses by a lot; convert into local space.
- Outlines or gradients creeping in → it turns into generic clip-art; keep blocks flat and outline-free.
- Uniform grain everywhere → looks like a dirty screen; let density follow light.
- Same coral/lilac/blob kit as every SaaS site → pick one unexpected colour pair and real acting to give it a voice.
